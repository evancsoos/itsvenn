**Prepared by:** Evan Soos

## 1. The One-Paragraph Pitch

Café Purg is a membership-driven café and late-night social space for the crowd nobody in Northwest Arkansas serves: people **between 18 and 21** — old enough to be adults, too young for every bar on the square. No alcohol, on purpose. Instead: serious coffee, a craft non-alcoholic beverage program (zero-proof cocktails, NA draft, house sodas), and a space that stays open after everything else closes. The name is the concept — purgatory between 18 and 21. The Owl's Roost loft gives us the one thing most café concepts die without: a space, on the square, with a landlord who wants the project to succeed.

## 2. Why This Works (and why now)

### The gap in the market (now verified venue-by-venue)
- We checked every venue within ~3 blocks of the square (`research/competitive-hours-matrix.md`): **after 9pm on a weeknight, every lit door on the square serves alcohol as the primary product.** The only all-ages non-alcohol venue open past 9 any night is the Spark Café soda fountain (weekends, to 10pm). A 17-year-old's realistic options at 9:30pm Saturday: one ice cream counter. Even First Friday — the marquee all-ages event — ends at 9.
- The customers exist and are growing: **Gallup (Aug 2025) has US drinking at a 90-year low — only 50% of 18–34s drink at all.** Locally, Bentonville's two high schools graduate **~1,400 new 18-year-olds every year**, NWACC is at record enrollment (10,700+), and its approved Bentonville campus master plan includes ~3,000 on-campus housing units.
- *(Honest flag: that's verified supply gap + favorable demographics, not measured demand — which is why pre-selling memberships is the load-bearing step in §7. The comp-set research in `research/demand-evidence.md` is blunt: standalone "sober bars" fail routinely; the survivors are **activity venues that happen to be alcohol-free**. Café Purg is positioned as exactly that — games, study hours, events, desserts — not as an "NA bar.")*
- The market is growing under us: Bentonville is Arkansas's fastest-growing city 2020–24 (+14%, ~62K people), Crystal Bridges drew 800K+ visitors in 2025 and just opened a 100,000 sq ft expansion, Walmart's new home office (capacity 15,000+) opened January 2025, and city events generated $41M in economic impact in 2024 — double the prior record. 90,000+ bike tourists come through annually.
- The non-alcoholic category is the fastest-growing in beverage: $823M off-premise in 2024 (+27% YoY, crossing $1B in 2025 per NielsenIQ), NA spirits +86% YoY, and ~49% of Americans planned to drink less in 2025. Mocktails sell for **$8–$14** with cocktail-like margins and zero licensing overhead.

### Why "no alcohol" is the feature, not the bug
- Arkansas's drinking age is 21, full stop — no membership or private-club structure changes that. Any concept serving 18–20 year olds **cannot** be built on alcohol, which means the bars on the square can't copy us without abandoning their license economics.
- Zero-proof drinks need **no ABC permit at all** — Arkansas alcohol law only applies above 0.5% ABV. Even NA beer (<0.5%) falls outside the statutory definition of "alcoholic beverage," so Arkansas law does not prohibit serving it to under-21 customers. *(We'll get written ABC confirmation before stocking NA beer — see §7.)*
- Late-night without alcohol is operationally calmer: lower insurance, no door liability, a space parents trust and 19-year-olds actually want.

### Why this space
- A family landlord removes the single biggest killer of first cafés: lease risk. **Market rent for ~1,000 sq ft near the square is $30–$36/sq ft/yr — $30,000–$36,000 a year** (current downtown listings; UA Skyline Report puts Bentonville Class A retail at $25–27/sq ft, overall retail vacancy just 3.4%). A favorable family lease is a quantifiable five-figure annual advantage — and steady lease income to Craig is more durable than Airbnb revenue.
- Second-floor loft = built-in "hideaway" character that matches the brand. The stairs are a real regulatory question (§4) but a brand asset.
- The bookstore downstairs is a complementary anchor: books + coffee + late-night lounge is a coherent block, and a shared ground-floor pickup counter is also our cleanest accessibility mitigation (§4).

## 3. The Business Model

### Revenue streams
1. **Walk-in café sales** (day): espresso program, pastries (baked off-site or ventless-reheated — a deliberate choice that saves ~$25–55K in buildout, §5), grab-and-go.
2. **Membership** (the core). Verified market precedents ladder cleanly: Panera Sip Club $15/mo → independent unlimited-drip clubs (Ula Cafe Boston, Morning Owl) $29/mo → espresso-inclusive programs (Pret $30–40, Blank Street $45–90/mo) → coworking hot desks ~$150/mo. Café Purg sits in the open middle:
   - **Purg Pass** (~$25/mo): members-only hours, free drip/tea on visits, event access.
   - **Resident** (~$50/mo): above + drink credits, guest passes, back-room booking priority.
   - 100 members at a $30 average = **$36,000/yr of recurring revenue** — roughly half the fixed operating floor (§6) covered before the first walk-in sale, and a buffer for the Dec–Feb trail-tourism slump.
3. **Events** (evening/late-night): open mics, game nights, watch parties, finals-week study halls, private bookings.
4. **Retail**: beans, NA bottles, merch.

> **Important distinction:** our "membership" is an ordinary business subscription. It has nothing to do with Arkansas's *private club* alcohol permit — we are not pursuing any alcohol permit at all.

### Concept guardrails
- 18–21 is the **identity and marketing wedge**, not a door policy. Everyone is welcome; the brand, hours, and programming aim at the under-served gap.
- **Positioning, per the comp research: a late-night activity venue that happens to be alcohol-free** — game library (evening cover, waived for members), study-hall passes, event nights, dessert program. The board-game-café literature warns that long dwell times + coffee-only tickets = "full room, barely breaking even," so the room itself is gently priced: evening per-head target is $15–22 (drink + dessert/snack + cover), vs. the $8–10 daytime ticket. Full priced menu: `concept-menu.md`.
- The menu is deliberately engineered to the cheapest compliant tier: beverage-forward, no fryer/grill/range, ventless cooking only. This single decision avoids a Type I hood system ($20–55K installed), keeps the health-inspection tier low, and may reduce the grease-trap requirement to an under-sink unit (§5).

### The alcohol option, researched and decided (`research/alcohol-option-abc.md`)
The question "does no-alcohol leave money on the table?" now has verified answers from the ABC Rulebook and Arkansas Code:
- **The under-21 brand and an alcohol permit are NOT legally incompatible.** Arkansas has no rule excluding under-21 patrons from a permitted *restaurant* — they can sit in the same room, at the same tables; 19–20-year-old staff can even serve beer/wine (not bartend). The legal wall we assumed doesn't exist.
- **The upgrade path is cheap and real:** Benton County is wet, the 1,000-ft church/school rule applies only to package stores (not on-premises restaurant permits), the block already holds five alcohol permits, and a **Restaurant Beer & Wine combo costs $350/yr** with no food-to-alcohol ratio in Arkansas law, beer/wine exempt from the mixed-drink supplemental taxes, and a 1–4 month process.
- **What staying dry costs:** a small beer/wine list typically adds 5–15% of revenue at ~75–80% (beer) / 60–70% (wine) gross margin — roughly **$25–45K/yr foregone gross profit** at base-case revenue. Material, not existential, and partially offset by 80–90%-margin zero-proof drinks.
- **Why we still open dry (Option A), with the A+ path documented:** an 18–21-branded venue serving alcohol is structurally a permanent ABC compliance test (sale-to-minor cases dominate ABC's violation résumés), the permit is discretionary ("public convenience and advantage" — a protest could cite our own branding), and dram-shop liability's minor-sale prong is exactly our crowd. The zero-proof program is also the only thing on this block the five licensed neighbors can't copy. **For Craig, the point is: the tenant is not structurally capped — if evening revenue data later justifies it, the $350 permit is exercisable in a season**, with ID-scan/wristband protocols and 100% staff certification (the Ocean Beach Café model: NA-forward menu, small real list, all-ages room).

## 4. Regulatory Path — City of Bentonville (verified June 2026)

**The headline: Bentonville adopted an entirely new development code effective May 14, 2026, and under it a café on the square is permitted by right — no rezoning, no conditional use permit, and no parking requirement.**

- **Zoning:** the square sits in the new **T5.2 Town Center High** district (to be confirmed for the exact parcel — Planning, 479-271-3122). "Food and beverage" is a by-right use in T5.2 per the adopted use matrix (Sec. 50-406, Table 4.2). **Minimum car parking for food & beverage in T5/T6: zero** (Table 5.9).
- **Process:** previously occupied space → **Commercial Alteration/Remodel Permit** via the city's eTrakit portal. Requires stamped drawings (occupancy load, travel distances, MEP), **ADH approval first** (§5), and a free annually-renewed **Business Registry** certificate. Plan review is unified under the Building & Fire Safety Division (Fire Marshal Todd Pearce, 479-271-3108).
- **The free first move:** a **Conceptual Meeting** with Planning (planning@bentonville.ar.gov / 479-271-3122), followed if needed by a Wednesday Pre-Application Conference. No fee for either. This converts every open question below into a written answer before a dollar is spent.
- **The design lever that controls everything — occupant load:**
  - Under the 2021 IBC (Arkansas's adopted code), a food-service space with **fewer than 50 occupants is Group B**, not Assembly. At 15 sq ft/person, that means keeping the dining area under ~700–750 net sq ft — natural for this loft.
  - Staying under 50 matters enormously: a second-floor **A-2** space (≥50 occupants) **automatically triggers building sprinklers** (IBC 903.2.1.2). Group B doesn't.
  - At **≤29 occupants**, a single code-compliant stair may legally suffice; at 30–49, two means of egress are required (an exterior steel egress stair runs ~$10–30K if needed). The existing stair situation is one of the two site questions to verify first.
- **Accessibility (the highest-risk item, stated honestly):** the 2010 ADA Standards exempt private buildings under 3 stories (or under 3,000 sq ft/story) from requiring an elevator — and DOJ's "shopping center" carve-out needs **five or more** sales establishments, which a bookstore + café doesn't meet. So an elevator is **plausibly not legally required** — but the IBC's accessible-dining provisions give the building official discretion, and we will not commit money until this is **confirmed in writing** by the building official and an Arkansas-licensed architect. Mitigation if it goes against us: a ground-floor service/pickup counter shared with the bookstore satisfies the "same services on an accessible level" principle; worst case, a vertical platform lift is ~$10–25K (a LULA elevator, $60–70K, is the scenario that kills the budget — we'd know before spending).
- **Also verified:** no historic design-review board exists (the new Downtown Center Overlay applies objective standards only if we touch the façade); a **1% A&P tax** applies to prepared food/NA beverages on top of ~9.5% sales tax; building permit fee for a $150K buildout ≈ **$730**.
- **Timeline:** pre-app → design + ADH plan review (4–8 wks) → city review (3–8 wks, unofficial estimate) → construction → CO. **Plan 4–7 months from engaging an architect to opening.**
- **Open items for the pre-app meeting:** parcel zoning confirmation; written accessibility determination; stair/egress survey; **structural check of the historic floor (assembly live load is 100 psf vs. 40 psf residential)**; sprinkler/alarm determination.

## 5. Regulatory Path — Arkansas Department of Health (verified June 2026)

- **Plan review is mandatory before construction** (Retail Food Rule § 8-201.10) — submit menu, layout, finish schedules, equipment specs, and SOPs through ADH's e-plan portal. Fee: **1% of project cost, capped at $500**. The annual food permit is **$35**. City permit approval requires ADH approval first, so this is sequenced early, not late.
- **The residential kitchen is a hard no** — § 6-202.20 categorically bars retail food operations in private homes or rooms opening into living/sleeping quarters. The loft must be fully converted; there is no shortcut, and the budget in §6 reflects that.
- **What the rules actually require physically:** a 3-compartment sink, hand sink(s) at the bar/prep area plus restroom, a mop/service sink, 41°F refrigeration, smooth/nonabsorbent finishes in wet areas, and hot water sized to demand. Equipment cost for the sink package itself is under $2K; the plumbing rough-in is the larger line (§6).
- **Menu strategy pays off here too:** espresso + ventless rapid-cook + cold prep means no Type I hood under the 2021 Arkansas Mechanical Code, a lower inspection tier, and likely a small under-sink grease trap rather than an in-ground interceptor — Bentonville's FOG program (Ordinance 2019-185) makes a written pretreatment determination cheap to get in advance (479-696-0293).
- **Staffing:** one **Certified Food Protection Manager** required (ServSafe, ~$150, 12-month grace period from permit issuance). Arkansas requires no food-handler cards.
- **Pilot option:** ADH explicitly permits operating from a licensed commissary/shared kitchen with a permission letter — meaning we can pre-sell memberships and pop-up at events *before* buildout to test demand.
- **Local contact:** Benton County Health Unit EHS, Rogers — (479) 986-1300.

- **Break-even**, at the verified 65–75% beverage gross margin: roughly **$9–18K/month of revenue** before rent — about **40–70 transactions a day at a $9 average ticket**. Benchmark small independents do 150–300/day.
- **Base case:** a modest 100–130 transactions/day ≈ **$330–430K/yr revenue**, squarely inside the verified small-café benchmark ($300–550K/yr, cross-checked across Toast, Roast Launch, and ticket×volume math). At benchmark margins that supports paying Craig real rent *and* a typical 2.5–10% net — and every membership dollar is on top.
- **The family-lease effect, quantified:** at market rent ($30–36K/yr), a benchmark café here is viable but ordinary. Each $10K/yr of rent relief moves ~3 points of net margin — the difference between an industry-average survivor and a comfortably profitable business that can reinvest in programming.
- **Honesty check:** roughly half of independent coffee shops don't survive five years (no rigorous primary source exists; best available analyses converge on ~50%). The mitigations are structural, not hopeful: recurring membership revenue, rent advantage, a defensible niche, and demand-testing before buildout.

## 6a. How It Gets Funded (`research/financing-grants.md`)

The model (`financials/`) shows break-even at 40–70 transactions/day and a slow-year stress test where 100 members turn five losing winter months into zero. The capital side, verified program-by-program:
- **The family LLC is the most powerful lever**: landlord-funded tenant improvements amortized into a market-rate rent (standard structure, ~7–8%) move $25–75K of fixed buildout off the loan request before it's sized. SBA's related-party rules are NOT a problem here — they bind only when SBA money buys the building; what's needed is a 10+ year market-rate lease (broker comp letter) and Craig's signature on a standard landlord waiver.
- **A sanity-checked $250K stack**: ~$60K founder/family equity (gift letters count toward SBA injection) + ~$100K SBA 7(a) via Arkansas Capital Corporation + ~$35K microloan (FORGE/Communities Unlimited) + equipment financing + **$15K Kiva NWA at 0% interest, credit-blind, Walton-matched** — the best first money for a thin-credit founder, and the campaign doubles as pre-marketing.
- **What not to budget**: Walton Family Foundation grants (nonprofits only — their money reaches founders through Kiva/EforAll/Startup Junkie, all free to use), dead pitch programs, façade grants (none exist). Free help that lenders respect: ASBTDC loan packaging, Startup Junkie consulting, EforAll NWA accelerator.
- **One approval lever to plan for**: 7(a) lenders discount first-time operators — naming an experienced café manager in the plan materially changes the odds.

## 7. The Plan — What Happens in What Order

1. **Pre-sell the concept** (now, ~$0): founding-member waitlist at $25–50/mo, Sans Bar-style $15-cover pop-up nights via a licensed commissary kitchen (explicitly allowed by ADH), Kiva campaign. Target: **75–100 committed founding members — this is Gate 1**, and per the demand research it is the only honest demand test that exists; no survey or dataset can substitute.
2. **Free conceptual meeting** with Bentonville Planning (479-271-3122) + Building & Fire Safety (479-271-3108), with Craig: confirm parcel zoning, get the accessibility and egress questions answered in writing, scope the structural check. Also: written grease-trap determination (479-696-0293) and written ABC confirmation on NA beverages.
3. **Go/no-go.** If elevator-required or structural surprise → stop, having spent almost nothing, with a fallback of ground-floor lease comps already researched.
4. **Engage an AR-licensed architect** ($1.5–6K) → ADH plan review → city permit → buildout. 4–7 months to opening.
5. **Lease terms with Craig** negotiated against real buildout numbers: e.g., reduced base rent + percentage rent, with shell improvements treated as landlord capital investment in the property.

### What we're asking Craig for
1. Agreement in principle to take the loft off Airbnb if the regulatory gates clear.
2. Two hours: attend the conceptual meeting together.
3. A lease structure conversation once buildout numbers are bid — not before.

## 8. Risks, Stated Honestly

| Risk | Severity | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Building official requires elevator or major structural work | **High — the go/no-go gate** | Written determination at pre-app before any spend; ADA exemption plausibly applies (verified rule text); ground-floor pickup counter as fallback; VPL ($10–25K) before LULA ($60–70K) |
| Single stair caps occupancy at 29 | Medium | Design to 29 first (still ~$330K/yr revenue capacity at benchmark turns); add egress stair (~$10–30K) only if demand proves out |
| 18–21 concept doesn't convert to memberships | **High — comp set says so** | Sober-bar comps fail when positioned as drink venues; we position as activity venue, price the room (covers/passes), and gate construction on a successful pre-sale. No churn benchmark exists for this demographic — Gate 1 creates our own data |
| Someone fills the night gap first (ex-Pressroom corner at 100 NW 2nd is vacant, successor unannounced) | Medium | Speed to pop-ups builds the audience before any competitor builds a room |
| Buildout exceeds lean budget | Medium | Ventless menu locks out the biggest overruns; phased buildout; landlord/tenant cost split |
| Bookstore conflict (noise, hours, shared egress) | Medium | Bring them in early; they're also the accessibility solution (shared pickup counter) and a natural cross-promotion partner |
| Dec–Feb seasonal slump | Medium | Membership base + finals-week and event programming |
| NA-beer-to-under-21 interpretation challenged | Low | Zero-proof program needs nothing; written ABC confirmation before stocking <0.5% products |
