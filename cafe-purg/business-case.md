## 1. The One-Paragraph Pitch

Café Purg is a membership-driven café and late-night social space for the crowd nobody in Northwest Arkansas serves: people **between 18 and 21** — old enough to be adults, too young for every bar on the square. No alcohol, on purpose. Instead: serious coffee, a craft non-alcoholic beverage program (zero-proof cocktails, draft NA options, house sodas), and a space that stays open after everything else closes. The name is the concept — purgatory between 18 and 21. The Owl's Roost loft gives us the one thing most café concepts die without: a space, on the square, with a landlord who wants the project to succeed.

## 2. Why This Works (and why now)

### The gap in the market
- Bentonville's square is saturated with daytime coffee (Onyx, Airship, et al.) and 21+ nightlife. There is **no third place for 18–20 year olds** — and very little for anyone after 9pm that isn't a bar.
- NWA's population is young and growing fast: NWACC, the University of Arkansas 30 minutes south, Walmart/supplier early-career hires, and the MTB/outdoor scene all skew 18–25.
- The sober-curious / non-alcoholic beverage trend is the fastest-growing segment in beverage. A zero-proof program isn't a compromise here — it's the product.

### Why "no alcohol" is the feature, not the bug
- Arkansas drinking age is 21, full stop. No membership or private-club structure changes that for 18–20 year olds. So any concept serving this age group **cannot** be built on alcohol — which means established bars can't copy us without abandoning their license economics.
- No liquor license means: no ABC permitting, lower insurance, no age-policing liability at the door beyond normal business judgment, and hours flexibility.
- Late-night without alcohol is operationally calmer: fewer incidents, lower security cost, a space parents are comfortable with and 19-year-olds actually want.

### Why this space
- **Family landlord** removes the single biggest killer of first cafés: lease risk. Market-rate retail near the square is expensive (figures in §6); a favorable family lease is a direct, quantifiable subsidy to the business — and rent Craig collects is more durable than Airbnb income if the concept holds.
- Second-floor loft = built-in "hideaway" character that matches the brand. The stairs are a real regulatory question (§4) but a brand asset.
- The bookstore downstairs is a complementary anchor, not a competitor: books + coffee + late-night lounge is a coherent block.

## 3. The Business Model

### Revenue streams
1. **Walk-in café sales** (daytime): espresso program, pastries (baked off-site to keep the kitchen requirements light — see §5), grab-and-go.
2. **Membership** (the core): monthly subscription sold on community and access, not discounts alone. Draft tiering:
   - **Purg Pass** (~$25/mo): members-only hours, free drip/tea on visits, event access, name on the wall.
   - **Resident** (~$50/mo): above + N drink credits, guest passes, booking priority for the back room.
   - Membership smooths the **December–February slump** in trail-tourism traffic with predictable recurring revenue and gives the 18–21 identity a tangible artifact (the card).
3. **Events** (evening/late-night): open mics, game nights, watch parties, study-hall finals weeks, private bookings.
4. **Retail**: beans, NA bottles, merch.

> **Important distinction for Craig:** our "membership" is a normal business subscription — it has nothing to do with Arkansas's *private club* alcohol permit, and we are not pursuing any alcohol permit at all.

### Concept guardrails
- 18–21 is the **identity and the marketing wedge**, not a hard door policy. Everyone is welcome; the brand, hours, and programming are aimed at the under-served gap. (A hard 18–21-only policy would shrink the market and add enforcement headaches for no benefit.)
- Menu is deliberately designed to stay in the lowest-cost health-permit tier: beverage-forward, no fryer, no grill, no Type I hood (see §5 — this is the single biggest buildout cost lever).

## 7. What We're Asking Craig For

1. **Agreement in principle** to take the loft off Airbnb for this project if the regulatory path clears.
2. **Joint attendance** at a pre-application meeting with Bentonville Planning & Development — free, and it converts every unknown in §4 from "maybe" to a written answer before a dollar is spent.

## 8. Risks, Stated Honestly

| Risk | Severity | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| City denies or heavily conditions change-of-use (esp. second-floor accessibility) | **High — go/no-go gate** | Pre-app meeting *first*, before any spend. Fallback: ground-floor space search (comps in §6). |
| Buildout cost exceeds what the revenue model supports | High | Menu designed to avoid hood/full kitchen; lean scenario in §6; phased buildout. |
| 18–21 concept doesn't convert to paying members | Medium | Membership is additive — the café works as a regular late-night café even if membership underperforms. Pre-sell founding memberships before buildout to test demand. |
| Bookstore conflict (noise, hours, shared egress) | Medium | Bring them in early as a stakeholder; align hours; soundproofing in buildout scope. |
| Seasonal slump (Dec–Feb) | Medium | Membership revenue base + events calendar + finals-week programming. |
| Airbnb income loss for Craig during buildout | Medium | Quantify against lease income in §6; rent abatement only during construction. |
