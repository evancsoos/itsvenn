# Research: The Alcohol Option — Arkansas ABC Analysis

Primary sources: ABC Rulebook (3 CAR, 01/14/2025, read directly), Ark. Code Title 3 (text read), AR Legislative Tax Handbook. June 2026.

## Location: no legal barrier at 116 W Central
- **Benton County wet since Nov 2012** (~66% yes; campaign led by Tom & Steuart Walton). Bentonville approved Sunday sales Nov 2022 (74%; Ord. 2023-081). HIGH.
- **Church/school 1,000-ft rule does NOT apply**: § 3-4-206 + 3 CAR § 1-202(6) restrict only retail liquor (package) stores and off-premises beer at liquor stores — NOT restaurant beer/wine/mixed-drink on-premises permits. ABC FAQ confirms. HIGH. (Churches can still *protest* any application → public hearing.)
- **Block precedent overwhelming**: Barley & Vine (111A W Central), Scotch & Soda (121 W Central), Yeyo's (122 W Central), Table Mesa, Butterscotch Bar — alcohol permits are routine on this exact block. MED.

## Under-21 rules — THE finding
- **Under-21 patrons may legally be present in a permitted RESTAURANT (beer/wine or mixed drink). No statute or ABC rule excludes them** — absence verified in both the full Rulebook and Title 3. The only minors-presence rule is 3 CAR § 5-404 (private clubs, and even there food service cures it). HIGH.
- An 18-year-old may sit at a table where others are served. Illegal = under-21 purchase/possession/consumption (§ 3-3-203) and permittee selling/furnishing/"allowing possession or consumption" (§§ 3-3-201/202). Mixed-age table sharing is exactly what ABC enforcement looks for. HIGH.
- **Staff: 19–20 year olds MAY serve beer/wine** (carry drinks, take payment) at restaurant-wine/mixed-drink/private-club permittees, but may NOT bartend (mixing = 21+). § 3-3-204. Note: beer-ONLY permit is not on the 19+ list — one more reason to take the $350 combo. HIGH.

## Operational deltas vs zero-proof
- **Dram shop**: AR civil liability for knowing sales to minors (§ 16-126-103) and to clearly-intoxicated (§ 16-126-104) — the minor prong is this venue's highest-frequency risk. Liquor liability insurance ~$250–$1,400/yr small café; 18–21 branding will push quotes up; ~15–20% discount for trained staff. HIGH/LOW-MED.
- **Server training not mandatory** in AR; Responsible Permittee Program (~$25/person) mitigates fines — treat as mandatory in practice, certify 100%. HIGH.
- **Honest sting-exposure assessment**: ABC violation résumés are dominated by sale-to-minor cases. A venue branded "we welcome 18–21" that serves alcohol is structurally a permanent compliance test, and the permit is discretionary — "public convenience and advantage" + "adequately served by existing outlets" is a ready-made denial theory for a protested application. Mitigations: ID-scan, wristbands, 100% certified staff, plan document that leads with food. HIGH on standard; MED on Board behavior (judgment).

## Hybrid precedent
- Ocean Beach Café (SF): zero-proof-forward menu + small real list, all-ages room — the flagship A+ model. Coffee-by-day/sober-by-night and time-based 21+ switches are house policy, not law, in AR. MED.
- Local: Butterscotch Bar (one block away) already pairs cocktails with a serious NA program. MED.

## Revenue math: what "no alcohol" costs
- Beer GM ~75–80%, wine ~60–70% (pour cost 18–24%). Alcohol ≈21% of sales at full-service but ~6% at limited-service; a café with small beer/wine list lands at 5–15% of revenue.
- **Illustrative: on $500K sales, 8–12% alcohol mix = $40–60K sales → ~$25–45K/yr incremental gross profit foregone** by staying dry — material, not existential; partially offset by 80–90%-margin zero-proof drinks. LOW (modeled).
