# Café Purg — Lease Term Sheet (Draft for Discussion)

**Landlord:** Owls Roast Loft LLC (Craig) · **Tenant:** Café Purg LLC (to be formed — never lease personally)
**Premises:** Second floor, 116 W Central Ave, Bentonville, AR — ~1,980 sq ft gross per parcel record, exact rentable square footage to be measured at drawing stage. Shared elements: street-level entry door and stairwell (exclusive), alley access (shared with bookstore).

> ⚠ This is a negotiation framework grounded in the project research — **not a lease and not legal advice.** A one-time review by an Arkansas commercial real estate attorney (~$1–2.5K) is in the soft-cost budget and is non-negotiable before signing, for Craig's protection as much as the café's.

---

## 0. Why the structure matters (the three masters)

1. **The SBA lender** (per `research/financing-grants.md`): lease term + *tenant-only* renewal options must run ≥ the loan term (10 years); rent must be **documented at market rate** (below-market rent inflates projections and gets re-underwritten anyway; above-market looks like disguised distributions); Craig must sign a standard **landlord's waiver/subordination** (lender gets notice of default, right to cure, access to remove equipment collateral) and consent to **collateral assignment of the lease**. All routine — but they must be agreed up front, so they're in this term sheet.
2. **Craig**: durable income that beats the hassle-adjusted Airbnb return, his building improved at a fair return on any capital he puts in, and clean exits if the café fails.
3. **The café**: survivable rent in the ramp year, no rent during construction, and contingency outs if the city kills the project.

## 2. Contingencies (lease voids cleanly, deposits returned, if any fail)

1. City of Bentonville approves change of use **without requiring an elevator** or structural work exceeding $___ (negotiated cap).
2. ADH plan-review approval.
3. Tenant secures financing of at least $___ by a date certain.
4. **Gate 1 language optional but honest:** tenant reaching 75 founding members by a date certain. (Craig may actually want this — it protects him from a tenant who can't fill the room.)

## 3. Rent — recommended structure (Option B), with alternatives

**Market anchor first** (this becomes the broker comp letter for the SBA file): downtown ground-floor retail runs $25–36/sq ft (Skyline Report + current listings); **second-floor walk-up space typically rents at a 40–60% discount to ground floor** → defensible market for this loft ≈ **$14–18/sq ft = $28K–$36K/yr ≈ $2,300–$3,000/mo**.

### Option B (recommended): market-documented base + percentage kicker
- **Base rent: $2,500/mo ($15.15/sq ft — documented market for second-floor)**, abated during construction, then **50% for months 1–6 of operations** ($1,250/mo ramp relief), full thereafter.
- **Percentage rent: 4% of gross sales above $350K/yr** — Craig participates in the upside he's enabling. At the base case (~$380K) that's ~$1,200/yr; in a strong year (~$550K) ~$8,000/yr.
- 2.5% annual escalator from year 3.

### Option C: TI-amortization add-on (stacks onto B if Craig funds buildout)
- Craig capitalizes shell improvements (§4) — say **$50K** — amortized into rent at **7.5% over the 10-year term = +$594/mo**.
- If the café fails, Craig keeps improvements that raised his appraised building value (currently $741,845 of improvement value on the county card — every permanent dollar adds to it).

### Option A (simplest, weakest for SBA): flat $2,000/mo gross
Fine for a handshake; an SBA underwriter will re-cast it to market anyway and it gives Craig no upside. Listed for completeness, not recommended.

**Worked example — Option B+C with $50K landlord TI, base case sales $380K/yr:**
$2,500 base + $594 TI + ~$100/mo average percentage rent ≈ **$3,194/mo = $38.3K/yr to Craig**, while the café's rent load stays ~10% of revenue (healthy; danger zone is >12–15%).

## 4. Who builds what (the TI split that makes sense)

| Landlord capitalizes (stays with building, raises appraisal) | Tenant funds (trade fixtures, leaves with tenant) |
|---|---|
| HVAC/electrical service upgrades | Espresso machine, grinders, brewers |
| ADA restroom | Refrigeration, ice, blenders, NA draft system |
| Fire alarm, egress stair if required | Furniture, millwork counter (negotiable) |
| Structural reinforcement if required | POS, smallwares, decor, signage face |
| Plumbing rough-in to the bar wall | Final fixture connections |

This split mirrors the SBA-friendly logic: the building owns what's bolted to it; the loan collateralizes what isn't.

## 5. Expenses (modified gross recommended over NNN for simplicity)

- **Property taxes** (~$11.9K/yr building-wide): tenant pays **pro-rata share (~50%) of increases above the base year** — not the whole bill. Craig was paying it before; the café shouldn't absorb the bookstore's half.
- **Building insurance**: Craig carries property; tenant carries GL ($1M/$2M) + contents + workers' comp, **landlord named additional insured**; certificate annually.
- **Utilities**: separately metered at buildout (cheap to do during electrical work; avoids every future argument).
- **Trash/grease/pest**: tenant's own contracts.

## 6. Term & options

- **Initial term: 10 years** (SBA minimum given a 10-year 7(a)).
- **Two 5-year renewal options, exercisable by tenant only** (SBA requires tenant-only).
- No early-termination right for landlord except uncured default; tenant gets a one-time **kick-out option at end of year 3** if gross sales < $200K trailing-twelve-months (honest failure exit — protects the family relationship more than either party).

## 7. Use, exclusivity, coordination

- Permitted use: café/restaurant, non-alcoholic beverage service, events, retail incidental. **Broad enough to add a beer/wine permit later without lease amendment** (the A+ option) — write it in now.
- Landlord exclusivity covenant: no other café/coffee use leased in the building (protects against a bookstore café counter competing upstairs/downstairs).
- Hours: tenant may operate to 1:00 a.m.; noise mitigation in buildout scope; quiet-hours protocol with bookstore documented in an exhibit.
- Signage: blade sign + window rights granted, tenant obtains permits.
- Bookstore ground-floor pickup counter (if the ADA mitigation is needed): documented as a license between the two tenants, rent-free, terminable on 90 days.

## 8. The SBA cooperation clause (put it in writing now)

Landlord agrees to execute, at tenant's lender's request: (a) landlord's waiver and lien subordination as to tenant's equipment; (b) consent to collateral assignment of lease; (c) estoppel certificates within 10 business days. Costs the family nothing; saves the loan.

## 9. Personal guarantee

Craig's LLC will reasonably want one from a first-time operator. Counter-structure: **limited guarantee burning off as the café performs** — e.g., guarantee capped at 12 months' rent, reducing to 6 months after two years of timely payment, gone after four. Standard, fair, and keeps family dinners pleasant.

## 10. Craig's side of the ledger, honestly

| | Airbnb (status quo) | Lease (Option B+C) |
|---|---|---|
| Gross | ~$245/night × ~60% occupancy ≈ **$53K/yr** | **~$38K/yr** |
| Costs | Cleaning/turnover/supplies/management/platform fees, typically 30–40% → **net ~$32–37K** | Near-zero management; tenant pays own utilities; **net ~$36–38K** minus his debt service on TI |
| Volatility | Seasonal, review-dependent, STR-regulatory risk | 10-year contract, percentage upside |
| Asset effect | Wear from turnover | **$50–150K of permanent improvements** at a 7.5% return on his capital |
| Hassle | Ongoing | A check that arrives |

The honest pitch: **roughly net-equivalent income, dramatically lower variance and effort, building value up, and a percentage of the upside** — plus his kid gets a business with a fair landlord instead of a fatal one.
